# Canggu, Bali: place research for the world bible

Date: 2026-11-12. Purpose: concrete facts about the real Canggu so the game district reads as Canggu and not a generic tropical island.

How to read this file:

- Plain bullets are backed by at least one source in the Sources list.
- "(unverified)" means I could not confirm it in a source during this research pass. Most of those are widely repeated common knowledge about Bali, but treat them as needing a check before they become canon.
- "(sources conflict)" means two sources disagree; both versions are given.
- Prices are in Indonesian rupiah (IDR) and come from travel blogs of mixed dates, so use them as rough scale only.

## 1. Geography and layout

### The coast in one picture

- Canggu is a strip of dark volcanic-sand beaches on Bali's south-west coast, facing south-west into the Indian Ocean. Every beach faces the sunset.
- One surf guide describes the core as "a 2 km strip of beach breaks and reefs over black sand".
- The sand is dark grey to black, from volcanic rock. It gets hot enough by late morning that guides tell people to wear sandals.
- Swimming is generally poor: strong waves, rip currents, shore break. One guide states there are no formal lifeguards at Batu Bolong. People wade, surf and sit; they do not really swim.
- At high tide the sand narrows and waves reach the cafés at Batu Bolong. At low tide dark reef platforms and tide pools are exposed.

### Beaches in order, south-east to north-west

The coast runs roughly from Seminyak (south-east) up to Tabanan (north-west). Guides call this direction "south to north" or "east to west" interchangeably.

1. **Batu Belig** (border of Seminyak/Kerobokan and Canggu). Lively, several small beach bars, one large beach club on the sand, popular for Sunday sunset. Free parking.
2. **Berawa** (Tibubeneng village). Wide, open beach. Two very large beach clubs (Finns, Atlas) dominate one stretch. Mixed crowd: families, party-goers, influencers. A guide says the sand here "encloses the largest lagoon along the Canggu coastline", and the beach ends to the north at a second lagoon where Nelayan begins. A river drains here and flushes dirty water into the surf after heavy rain. Parking IDR 5,000-10,000.
3. **Nelayan** ("fisherman" beach, end of Jalan Nelayan). Calm and quiet. Traditional fishing boats pulled up on the sand, a few simple warungs and beach bars, locals fishing in late afternoon. Waves are messy and inconsistent; kite surfers appear when the wind picks up. Parking IDR 5,000.
4. **Batu Bolong** (end of Jalan Pantai Batu Bolong). The busiest beach and the social centre. Beginner surf, dozens of board-rental shacks, surf schools, rented loungers, bars and cafés along the back of the beach, a nightlife strip behind it. Parking IDR 5,000 in small lots run by local attendants.
5. **Old Man's**. A surf break and a large beer-garden bar of the same name at the Batu Bolong end; in practice it is the same beach area as Batu Bolong.
6. **Batu Mejan / Echo Beach**. Guides say there is "no clear border" between Batu Mejan and Echo Beach. More of a surfer crowd, punchier waves, cafés on the shore, a large bohemian beach club (La Brisa) with a Sunday market. Mellow on the sand. Parking IDR 5,000.
7. **River mouth**. A river reaches the sea between Echo Beach and Pererenan and separates Canggu from Pererenan. A named surf break ("River Mouth") sits here. Water is murky with debris after rain. The river's name is unconfirmed (one address nearby reads "Jalan Tukad Pingai"). (river name unverified)
8. **Pererenan** (Mengwi district). Lonely Planet: an easy 300 m walk west from Echo Beach across sand and rock formations, or about 1 km by road. Wide, quieter, a few low-key warungs and minimal beach bars. Landmark: a 12 m statue on a rock platform of the sea god Baruna riding Gajah Mina (an elephant-headed fish). A surf break sits right in front of that rock. Parking IDR 5,000.
9. **Babadan, Munggu, Seseh, Cemagi** (further north-west). Progressively emptier: fishermen, solo surfers, local families, almost no vendors. A small river at the west end of Cemagi marks the start of Tabanan regency. Good "edge of the map" material.

Notes on conflicts:

- One guide lists a tiny "Munduk Catu Beach, beside The Lawn" between Echo and Pererenan. The Lawn is normally placed at Batu Bolong, so this position looks wrong. (sources conflict)

### Temples on or near the beach

- **Pura Batu Bolong**: a small, active Hindu temple at Batu Bolong beach; the beach is named after it. One source describes it as sitting on a rocky outcrop at the southern end of the sand and predating Canggu's development by centuries. Visitors may look from outside the walls; the inner compound needs temple dress and is for worshippers. Rocks near it are sharp and uneven.
- One source says Pura Batu Bolong is "also called Pura Batu Mejan". My understanding is that Pura Batu Mejan is a separate temple at Echo Beach, which is why that beach is also called Batu Mejan. (unverified; sources conflict)
- Ceremonies are frequent and follow the Balinese 210-day Pawukon calendar. On ceremony days people in full temple dress cross the beach.
- **Melasti**: the purification procession before Nyepi (Balinese new year, usually March). Devotees carry the deities' thrones (jempana) and sacred objects to the sea at Batu Bolong, float offerings of petals, leaves and small money, and are sprinkled with holy water. A 2025 report covers the Badung regent leading one at Batu Bolong.

### Roads

- **Jalan Raya Canggu**: the main inland artery between Berawa and Batu Bolong. One of the busiest roads, patchy footpaths, guides say do not walk it.
- **Beach roads** run from that artery down to the sea like the teeth of a comb: Jalan Pantai Berawa (ends at the beach by a beachfront hotel), Jalan Nelayan, Jalan Pantai Batu Bolong (market at the top, beach at the bottom), Jalan Batu Mejan (to Echo Beach), and the Pererenan beach road. (comb layout is from general map knowledge; check against a map before modelling)
- Because the beach roads do not connect to each other near the coast, going from one beach to the next by road means going inland and back out. That is why the shortcut exists and why 300 m along the sand can be 1 km or more by road.
- **Traffic peaks**: 07:00-09:00, 16:00-19:00, and around sunset. The Berawa beachfront near the two big clubs jams at sunset and after events.
- Walking times: upper Batu Bolong to the beach 30-45 minutes; Batu Bolong to Berawa 20-40 minutes.

### The Canggu Shortcut

- A narrow road through what were rice paddies, linking Berawa and Batu Bolong. One source gives its ends as Jalan Subak Canggu and Jalan Anggrek.
- History: originally a farm path usable only by motorbike; repaved and slightly widened by late 2021; widened again since.
- Rule: one-way for cars (from the Canggu/Batu Bolong side toward Berawa); scooters go both ways.
- About 15 minutes end to end without traffic; in the afternoon it "turns into a car park" with scooters squeezing around gridlocked cars.
- It is famous for cars and scooters tipping off the edge into the paddies; a viral before/after clip showed how much it changed in ten years. (the tipping-into-paddies detail is widely reported but I did not confirm it in a source this pass: unverified)
- Today (article dated Dec 2025): the rice fields along it have largely gone, replaced by high walls, luxury villas and commercial space. Subak irrigation channels are dry or redirected in many places.
- Other shortcuts: the Umalas shortcut toward Seminyak (narrow, clogs fast); a newer link opened in 2024 between Jalan Raya Canggu and Jalan Raya Kerobokan; a Berawa-Umalas road reported due around December 2026.
- Drivers also cut along rice-paddy tracks to dodge jams; some inland tracks are scooter-only.

### Rice fields versus built-up

- Built up: Berawa, Batu Bolong, the shortcut corridor, Jalan Raya Canggu. Reviewers call Canggu "one giant building site" with construction audible nearly everywhere.
- Rice fields remain mostly at the edges: Pererenan, Seseh, Cemagi, Nyanyi, and pockets inland to the north. Listings there sell "rice-field edge" quiet.
- Good game framing: paddies are now pockets seen between villa walls, not the dominant landscape, and they get more common the further north-west you go.

## 2. Surf

### Breaks, south-east to north-west

| Break | Bottom / shape | Level | Tide notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Berawa: The Peak | A-frame | All levels on small days | Mid tide, works on most |
| Berawa: The Ledge | Fast shallow right over reef | Experienced only | Low tide, bigger swell |
| Berawa: The Bombie | Soft peaks far out | Big boards, paddleboards | Needs solid swell |
| Nelayan | Submerged rock shelf, deep | Often flat or shore break | Inconsistent |
| Batu Bolong | Soft, slow, peels 100 m+ both ways | Beginners, longboarders | Mid to high (sources conflict; one says low to mid) |
| Old Man's | Deeper reef with a clear channel, easy paddle-out | Beginners | Mid (sources conflict) |
| Echo Beach Left | Fast, steep left over shallow shelf | Intermediate to advanced | Mid to high; low exposes reef |
| The Sandbar | Sandy A-frame, shifting peaks | Advanced | Low or mid-high (sources conflict) |
| River Mouth | Right-hand wedge at river mouth | Intermediate to advanced | High tide |
| Pererenan | Rock and reef, left and right | Intermediate (the left is hazardous) | Mid; low exposes reef |

- Crowd character: Batu Bolong is "a dense mix of surf schools and loggers". Old Man's is the relief valve. Berawa has room. Echo is competitive. Pererenan is the overflow when Echo is packed.
- At low tide Batu Bolong closes out and is unsuitable for beginners.

### Rental and schools on the sand

- "Dozens of surf shacks scattered along the beach" at Batu Bolong rent boards and rash vests/wetsuits.
- Soft-top (foam) board rental: IDR 100,000-150,000 per day; cheaper per day for several days.
- Lessons: 90 minutes to 2 hours; one blog quotes about US$25 for two hours.
- Surf schools run out of beachfront venues; students are in the water by about 08:00.
- Typical look (unverified against a source, but standard across Bali): racks of colourful foam boards stood upright in the sand, a hand-painted price board, instructors in matching rash vests, students practising pop-ups on boards laid flat on the sand before going in.

### Time of day

- Before 09:00 is best: light offshore wind, cleanest waves, fewest people.
- 06:30-08:00 is the calmest window. 07:00-10:00 the lineup fills. By 10:00 the beach is full.
- South-east trade winds usually arrive around 10:00 and blow side-shore until they drop near sunset. There is a second busy session from about 15:00.

### Seasons and tide

- Dry season (April/May to October): consistent Indian Ocean swell, offshore mornings, cleaner water. The main surf season.
- Wet season (November to March): onshore wind from the west or south-west, smaller and choppier, river runoff. Mornings are often still glassy.
- Best swell direction is south-west; best wind is a light north-easterly.
- Tidal range observed at about 3 m on two logged days, which is large enough that the same beach looks very different at high and low tide.

## 3. Beach life by hour

### Sunrise (about 05:50-06:35 depending on month)

- The sun rises behind the land, so the beach is in soft, even light, not a sunrise over water.
- Surfers paddling out, dog walkers, runners, a few people doing yoga or pilates. A big beach club ran a "sunrise pilates" on Berawa beach at 07:00-09:00 in Sept 2025.
- Run clubs meet at 06:15-07:00.
- Rental shacks and warungs setting up. Morning offerings being placed (see section 5).

### Mid-morning to midday

- Surf schools in full swing from 08:00; beach full by 10:00.
- Sand too hot to walk barefoot by late morning. People retreat under umbrellas, into cafés, or into beach-club pools.
- Rented loungers under umbrellas: about IDR 150,000 for the day, negotiable for an hour or two.

### Late afternoon and sunset (about 18:05-18:40)

- The main event. Crowds build from about 17:00 until dark. One guide calls Batu Bolong "Canggu's open-air theatre" at this hour.
- The sun sets straight out over the water. Arrive 30-60 minutes early to get a seat.
- On the sand: bean bags and low tables, coconuts and Bintang beer, surfers on the last waves, people flying kites, stray and pet dogs playing, families, couples' photo shoots.
- Bars bring in DJs or live music by early evening.
- Locals fish at Nelayan in the late afternoon.
- Grilled-corn carts and horse riding on the sand are often mentioned for this coast, but I found no source for either. (unverified)

### Night

- The strip behind Batu Bolong (a beer-garden surf bar and a Mexican-themed bar are the best known) becomes the nightlife zone with DJs and late opening.
- One Berawa club closes at midnight and has a nightclub on the same site.
- Ride-hailing is hard to get right at the beach after sunset; people walk a few hundred metres inland to get picked up. Hardest window to leave Canggu is midnight to 03:00.
- A petition and older articles complain about bar noise into the early morning.

### Beach clubs (as a type)

- Large walled compounds on the beachfront. Entry is usually free; you pay through a **minimum spend** attached to the seat you choose.
- Seat ladder: free standing or bar space, then tables, then pool loungers, then daybeds, then cabanas or a private tower for groups. Minimum spends range from about IDR 100,000-350,000 (casual clubs) to IDR 2,500,000-3,200,000 (premium daybeds at the biggest clubs), often with part returned as food-and-drink credit.
- Features: infinity pool facing the ocean, swim-up bar, multiple bars (the biggest has eleven), DJ booth, lockers for a small fee, food delivered to the bed.
- Rhythm: opens around 10:00; daybeds gone by lunchtime on weekends; live acts from about 14:00; DJ from about 16:00; sunset peak; some run late.
- Styles vary: mega-club with pool parties; bohemian driftwood-and-reclaimed-boat style with bean bags on the sand; calm lawn-and-infinity-pool style; resort-attached quiet club; rooftop sunset bar.
- One club offers free entry before 14:00 with a physical passport, IDR 300,000 after.
- Real reference names only: Finns, Atlas, La Brisa, The Lawn, COMO Beach Club, Café del Mar, Sol Rooftop (Pererenan), Mari (Batu Belig).

### Beach warungs and bean-bag bars (as a type)

- Simple local stalls at the back of the sand selling nasi goreng, mie goreng, coconuts and beer; "quick and affordable". Café meals nearby run IDR 50,000-120,000.
- Bean bags, low tables and umbrellas set directly on the sand, facing west, filling up before sunset.
- Typical look (unverified against a source): bamboo-and-thatch or tarp roof, a chest cooler or fridge of beer, plastic chairs, coloured bean bags laid out in rows in the afternoon and stacked away at night, a hand-painted menu board.

## 4. Outdoor and beach gyms, training spots

### Direct answer

- **Open-air gyms are real and common in Canggu.** Several well-known gyms have an outdoor or semi-outdoor training floor: a roof, no walls, fans instead of air conditioning.
- **A public gym on the sand in Canggu: I could not verify one.** One guide lists a "Seashore Park" in Berawa with a "raw and rustic beachfront setup" plus café, sauna and cold plunge, but its address clashes with other gyms and no second source confirms it. (unverified)
- **Beach calisthenics bars exist elsewhere in south Bali.** A calisthenics directory lists a spot at Legian Beach (pull-up bars, parallel bars, push-up bars, low bars), one in Jimbaran (bars, plus weights and boxing gear for a small fee, toilets nearby), and a large bar park at Lapangan Puputan Badung in Denpasar (pull-up, parallel, monkey and wall bars). None listed in Canggu.
- So a beachfront outdoor gym in the game is a believable invention that borrows from real things nearby, but it is not a literal Canggu landmark.

### What the real open-air gyms look like

- An outdoor training area beside an air-conditioned indoor room, used for powerlifting and calisthenics.
- Open-air design with tropical planting and natural wood; one uses rattan and curved roofs.
- "Open-air gym with fans but no air conditioning."
- Functional zone with a rig ("jungle gym") and a strip of turf for sled pulls.
- One has a big open floor looking out over rice terraces.
- **Recovery area is part of the standard package**: a lap pool (about 20 m), day beds, sauna, cold plunge tubs (one advertises 5°C), hot tub, and a café.
- Prices seen: day pass IDR 200,000-550,000 at the premium places; a basic local gym IDR 30,000 a day.
- Real reference names only: Body Factory, Nirvana Life/Nirvana Strength (Berawa), Omni (Munggu), Elite Fit (Pererenan), Bambu Fitness, Wrong Gym, Bull Gym (Pererenan).

### Running, bootcamps, clubs

- Beach running works at low tide: Batu Bolong has "firm sandy stretches at low tide that are surprisingly runner-friendly". At high tide the sand is narrow and soft.
- Run clubs: free community runs at 06:15 (weekdays) and 07:00 (Saturday) with 4 km or 8 km loops; another at 06:30 Mondays from a Pererenan café with 3 km or 6 km; a coffee-shop club in Seseh by the rice fields; an evening one at 17:00 from a CrossFit gym.
- A beach bootcamp group ran 07:00-08:00 Monday/Wednesday/Friday on Batu Bolong beach: interval circuits on the sand. The schedule comes from an older guide. (current status unverified)
- Padel courts are now bundled with gym and recovery passes in Canggu.

## 5. Streets

### What sources confirm

- Scooters are the default transport. Reviewers call the area "barely walkable". Scooters ride up onto the few pavements when the road is blocked.
- Footpaths are inconsistent; the best are near the beach ends of Batu Bolong, Berawa and Pererenan.
- Roads are narrow because they were farm lanes; cars meeting head-on cause standoffs while scooters filter around them.
- Scooter rental: IDR 70,000-150,000 per day. Common models: Honda Scoopy and Vario (small), Yamaha NMAX and Honda PCX (bigger).
- Ride-hailing: Gojek and Grab, both scooter-taxi and car, IDR 15,000-40,000 for local trips. Drivers wear green jackets and helmets. (jacket colour unverified this pass)
- Drivers avoid picking up right at Batu Bolong beach and at the Berawa club entrances; passengers walk inland first.
- Bluebird taxis: light blue, metered.
- Parking: small lots near beach access, attendants collect IDR 5,000 (scooter). Parking is tight around Batu Bolong and the clubs.
- Petrol: subsidised Pertalite is IDR 10,000 per litre at official Pertamina stations. Roadside resale of petrol in bottles exists but is contested: one 2026 report describes stations refusing to fill bottles and police detaining resellers during a fuel shortage with multi-hour queues.
- Offerings: canang sari (small palm-leaf trays of flowers) are placed daily at home and shop entrances, workplaces and intersections; they are "all over the streets".
- Penjor: tall decorated bamboo poles lining streets during festivals (Galungan and Kuningan).
- Small street-side shrines and temples are scattered through every neighbourhood.
- Warungs: simple roadside eateries, food carts on wheels, and motorbikes fitted with a gas cooker.
- A daily market (Love Anchor) at the top of Jalan Pantai Batu Bolong sells clothes, jewellery and crafts; bargaining expected.
- Construction is constant; villa break-ins are a recurring complaint in nomad reviews.

### Common-knowledge texture I did not source this pass (all unverified)

- "Pertamini" mini pumps and shelves of petrol in reused glass spirit bottles outside small shops.
- "Laundry" shops charging by the kilo, bags of washing on a scale.
- Scooter-rental stands: a row of identical scooters with a hand-painted "RENT SCOOTER / MOTOR BIKE" sign and a WhatsApp number.
- Scooters with surfboard racks on the side.
- Free-roaming "Bali dogs" asleep in the road; chickens and roosters; roosters kept under upturned woven bamboo baskets.
- Tangled overhead power and phone cables; open concrete drainage channels at the road edge; broken paving slabs.
- Villa walls: grey volcanic stone or rendered walls 2-3 m high with a carved wooden door or split gate, a small shrine niche by the entrance, frangipani and bougainvillea over the top.
- Villa style: open-sided living room facing a small pool, alang-alang thatch or terracotta-tile roofs, newer builds in white "Mediterranean" or raw-concrete style.
- Cafés: open-fronted, polished concrete, rattan lamps, laptops, smoothie bowls, rows of scooters parked nose-in outside.
- Signage: "villa for rent", "land for lease", money changers, tattoo studios, minimarts (Indomaret, Alfamart, Circle K), property agents.
- Black-and-white checked cloth (poleng) wrapped around shrines, statues and big trees.
- Banjar hall (bale banjar): an open community pavilion in each neighbourhood with a wooden slit-drum (kulkul) in a small tower.

## 6. Climate

- **Dry season**: roughly April/May to October. Slightly cooler and less humid. About 21-30°C. August is the driest month (about 2 inches / 50 mm of rain in Denpasar).
- **Wet season**: roughly November to March/April. About 23-32°C and more humid. Denpasar rainfall: December about 10 in (260 mm), January about 13 in (330 mm), February about 10 in, March about 9 in.
- **Daily pattern in the wet season**: mornings are usually dry and usable; rain comes in short, violent bursts in the afternoon or overnight and clears quickly. Showers still happen in the dry season, just rarer and lighter.
- **Daily wind pattern in the dry season**: still, glassy mornings; trade wind from about 10:00; wind drops around sunset.
- **Daylight barely changes**: shortest day about 11 h 37 min (21 June), longest about 12 h 38 min (21-22 December).
  - Late June: sunrise about 06:33, sunset about 18:10.
  - Late December: sunrise about 05:59, sunset about 18:37.
  - Earliest sunrise about 05:49 (mid November); latest about 06:36 (mid July). Sunset range across the year about 18:07-18:47.
- Time zone: WITA, UTC+8, no daylight saving.
- Twilight is short this close to the equator: it goes from sunset to full dark in well under half an hour. (unverified this pass, but follows from the latitude of about 8.6° S)
- **Wet-season beach trash**: from late November to early April, currents and west winds push plastic and driftwood onto Bali's south-west beaches. Worst at Kedonganan, Jimbaran, Kuta and Legian; Canggu is less affected but not spared. Around 20 tonnes a day has been cleared from the worst beaches. Crews with rakes and trucks, and volunteer clean-ups, are a real seasonal sight.
- River mouths run brown after rain and surfers avoid them.

## 7. Sounds and small details

### Confirmed by sources

- Roosters from about 05:30, every day. Villa listings tell guests to bring earplugs "for the frogs and roosters".
- Frogs and insects at night, loudest near rivers and paddies.
- Geckos indoors (hosts warn guests they will see them).
- Construction noise through the day: the single most consistent complaint.
- Scooter engines, dogs barking, ceremonies.
- Bar and club bass at night near Batu Bolong and Berawa.
- DJs and live music from beachfront bars from early evening.
- Surf: with a south-west-facing open coast and shore break, wave noise is constant on the sand.

### Common knowledge, not sourced this pass (all unverified)

- Gamelan (bronze metallophones and gongs) drifting from a temple or banjar hall during ceremonies and evening rehearsals.
- Kulkul: the hollow wooden drum beaten in a banjar tower to call the community.
- Tokay gecko: a loud, slow "to-kay ... to-kay" call repeated several times, heard at night. Small house geckos make a quick "chk-chk-chk".
- Short polite double-tap scooter horn used to say "I am passing", not anger.
- Parking attendants' whistles.
- Food-cart sounds: a spoon tapped on a bowl or a wooden block for the meatball-soup (bakso) cart.
- Crackle of offerings' incense smoke at doorways in the morning; smell of incense, clove cigarettes, frangipani, two-stroke exhaust, burning rubbish and satay smoke.
- Kites humming high overhead in the dry-season wind (large traditional kites carry a vibrating bow).
- Nyepi: one day a year (March) the whole island goes silent and dark: no traffic, no lights, airport closed. The night before, giant demon effigies (ogoh-ogoh) are paraded with gamelan and torches.

## Most game-usable details

1. **Black sand, west-facing coast.** Dark grey-black sand that shimmers with heat at midday; the sun always sets over the water and rises behind the land.
2. **The comb layout.** One busy inland road with dead-end beach lanes running down to the sea. Next beach is 300 m along the sand but 1 km+ by road, so walking the beach is the real shortcut.
3. **The Shortcut.** A single narrow raised lane with a drop on each side, one-way for cars, two-way for scooters, permanently jammed in late afternoon, now walled in by villas with a few leftover paddy pockets.
4. **Tide changes the map.** About 3 m of range: at high tide the sand is a thin strip and waves reach the café steps; at low tide wide firm sand, black reef shelves and tide pools appear, and runners come out.
5. **Beach temple on rock at Batu Bolong**, with processions in white and yellow temple dress crossing the sand between surfers on ceremony days.
6. **The Gajah Mina statue at Pererenan**: a 12 m sea god riding an elephant-headed fish on a rock platform, with surfers directly in front. A natural landmark for navigation.
7. **River mouth between Echo and Pererenan** that you wade across at low tide; runs brown with debris after rain.
8. **Nelayan fishing beach**: painted outrigger boats hauled up on the sand, men fishing at dusk, a quiet gap between two busy beaches.
9. **Surf-rental shacks**: racks of upright foam boards, price board (IDR 100-150k per day), lesson groups doing pop-up drills on the sand at 08:00.
10. **Surf day rhythm**: dawn glass and locals, 08:00 surf-school swarm, wind arrives around 10:00, lull at midday, second session from 15:00, last waves in silhouette at sunset.
11. **Sunset ritual at 17:00-18:40**: rows of bean bags and low tables facing west, coconuts and Bintang, kites, dogs chasing each other, a DJ starting up, then dark within half an hour.
12. **Beach club as a tiered space**: free to walk in, pay by where you sit; infinity pool on the ocean edge, swim-up bar, daybeds, cabanas, DJ from 16:00.
13. **Open-air gym kit**: roof and no walls, fans, a pull-up rig, a turf strip with a sled, racks and plates, then a recovery corner with sauna barrel, cold plunge tub, lap pool, day beds and a café counter. Legian-style beach pull-up and parallel bars are the real-world precedent for bars on the sand.
14. **Morning movement**: run-club packs at 06:15-07:00, bootcamp circuits on the sand at 07:00, pilates mats on the beach in front of a club.
15. **Scooters everywhere**: Scoopy/Vario/NMAX, surfboard side racks, nose-in rows outside cafés, scooters mounting the pavement to pass a stuck car, ride-hail drivers waiting a few hundred metres inland because they cannot pick up at the beach.
16. **Parking attendants** at each beach lot taking IDR 5,000 and handing over a paper ticket.
17. **Offerings underfoot**: fresh canang sari at every doorway and junction each morning, with incense; penjor bamboo arches over the lanes in festival weeks; small shrines on corners.
18. **Construction as ambience**: half-built villas behind bamboo scaffolding, grinders and hammering all day, "land for lease" boards in the last paddies.
19. **Dawn soundscape**: roosters from 05:30, dogs, the first scooters, surf in the distance.
20. **Night soundscape**: frogs and insects near paddies and rivers, geckos on the walls, bass from the beach bars carrying inland.
21. **Wet-season day**: clear morning, clouds stacking after lunch, a short violent downpour, steaming roads, brown river plumes in the sea, driftwood and plastic lines on the sand with clean-up crews raking.
22. **Dry-season day**: cooler clear dawn, steady afternoon breeze, kites up, dust instead of puddles, biggest and cleanest swell.
23. **Petrol**: official station price IDR 10,000 a litre; roadside bottles as a contested grey-market prop.
24. **Heat as a mechanic**: sand too hot to cross barefoot from late morning to mid-afternoon; shade, umbrellas and pools are where people actually are at midday.
25. **Edges of the map**: beyond Pererenan the beaches empty out (Seseh, Cemagi) into rice fields, fishermen and picnicking families: a natural low-density boundary and a hook for later districts.
