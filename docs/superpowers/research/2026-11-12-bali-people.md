# Canggu, Bali: people and scenes (world-bible research)

Researched 2026-11-12 from web sources (2024-2026 preferred). Prices are in Indonesian rupiah (IDR; "k" = thousand, "m" = million; roughly IDR 16,000-16,500 per USD). Anything marked **(unverified)** is either from a single weak source or from general knowledge I could not confirm on a page I read. Sources are numbered and listed at the end.

How to read confidence:
- No mark = found on a page I read, or in two or more sources.
- **(single source)** = one blog or listing; treat as approximate.
- **(unverified)** = not confirmed; safe as flavour, not as fact.

## 1. Entrepreneur and digital-nomad scene

### Who is there
- A named-event description from the island's main tech meetup lists its crowd as founders, digital nomads, agency owners, tech investors, ex-big-tech engineers, developers, solopreneurs, "indiehackers", local entrepreneurs and even government representatives [1].
- A 2024 Jakarta Post feature profiles a remote project manager for a Danish start-up, a 24-year-old social-media-agency owner and a 78-year-old New Yorker starting an online yoga-clothing shop, so the range of ages and jobs is wider than the cliché [2].
- Crypto is a real sub-scene. A 2024 Cointelegraph feature describes crypto tax-software managers, educators, consultants and anonymous traders working from Canggu, a Bitcoin meetup where attendees can ask for a sticker over their face in photos, and people introducing themselves by their X handle. Interviewees also warned of "the odd dodgy person" and pump-and-dump operators [3].
- Reviewers on Nomads.com say the population has shifted: a few years ago you had to quit your job or start a business to live there; now many are remote employees of multinationals. Others call the expat scene influencer-heavy and transient [4].
- In 2022 the then tourism minister said Canggu had the most digital nomads in Bali, mostly from Russia, the US and the UK [2].
- Other types commonly reported: e-commerce and dropshipping sellers, online coaches, content creators, and SaaS founders **(unverified as proportions; no source gives numbers)**.

### Coworking spaces
- Names that recur across guides: **Tropical Nomad** (open 24/7, café, strong community), **BWork Bali** (opened 2021, Jl. Nelayan 9C; runs yoga, boxing and Muay Thai wellness sessions), **Outpost**, **Tribal** (a café-style space where you spend about 200k a day to use it; the crypto hub in 2024), **Zin Cafe**, and **Dojo Bali** (Echo Beach; listed in many guides, current status unconfirmed) [2][3][5][6].
- Hybrid "club" spaces are the newer pattern: **Saya Club** combines gym, recovery (saunas, plunge pools) and coworking with meeting rooms, staffed 6:00-22:00; **Bali Social Club** combines padel, gym, coworking and a restaurant [7][8].
- Prices: Nomads.com lists a hot desk at about USD 192 a month; guides give day passes of IDR 230-260k and monthly passes from IDR 1.2-3.5m depending on hours [4][5] **(sources disagree; approximate)**.
- Community managers are usually young Indonesians. Tropical Nomad's community manager told the Jakarta Post the space runs regular meetups and workshops and helps newcomers find their group [2].
- A day inside one **(composite, partly unverified)**: quiet air-conditioned room with standing desks and phone booths; a louder café zone; a pool or garden; a noticeboard or WhatsApp group with the week's events; lunch ordered to the desk; a "skill share" or founder talk in the early evening.

### Events and how people meet
- **Bali Start-ups and Tech Community (Meetup)**: monthly "Networking Drinks & Dinner" in Canggu, numbered past #35. Example: Thursday 16 April 2026, 6:30-8:30 pm, Yema Kitchen, free entry. Loose format: mostly mingling, a quick open mic, a few short talks from founders or investors. It also runs a "How I AI" series where builders demo for about 10 minutes each plus questions [1].
- **Founder masterminds** at BWork: small founder-to-founder circle sharing wins and problems; 11 people attended one listed session [9].
- **Founders Padel & Networking** at Bali Padel Academy: padel matches, then sunset networking, sold as a curated monthly founders community (one edition SGD 30, another free via sponsors) [10].
- **Small curated dinners**: a "Female Founder Dinner" in Canggu (June 2026) was pitched as eight founders at one long table with no panels or small talk. **EDGE** runs masterminds and founder dinners, mostly in Uluwatu, plus a month-long "lock-in" in Canggu [11].
- No dedicated Canggu "pitch night" was found; demo-style talks happen inside the meetups above **(pitch nights unverified)**.
- Other meeting channels: run clubs, gym classes, padel open matches, WhatsApp and Facebook groups, and the Nomads.com meetup page [4][8][12].

### Working hours across time zones
- Bali is on WITA, UTC+8, with no daylight saving.
- Europe: Central European mornings start at 3-4 pm Bali time. The Jakarta Post profile describes brunch at 10 am and a work start at 2 pm to match Danish hours [2].
- US: East Coast 9 am is 9-10 pm in Bali, so US-facing people take calls at night or very early morning. A founder's write-up noted that working late leaves evenings short while most of Bali's life happens in daylight [13].
- Australia and Singapore overlap almost fully; India is 2.5 hours behind, so Indian clients come online around 11:30 am-12:30 pm Bali time.
- Result: mornings are free for training and surfing, and coworking spaces are busiest from late morning to evening.

### Typical conversations and in-jokes
- Traffic and "the shortcut" (a narrow rice-field lane between Berawa and Canggu that jams) are a running joke among nomads; a commentary notes locals live with the same roads as a daily grind, not a joke [14].
- "Bali belly" stories; whose villa has mould or a roof leak; which gym has the coldest plunge; padel ratings; visa-agent recommendations; "how long are you here for?" [4][8] **(framing unverified, themes sourced)**.
- Jokes that everyone is a coach, a founder or "building something", and that people leave "next month" for years **(unverified; common trope in reviews)**.

### Visa basics (flavour only, not advice)
- Visa on Arrival (30 days, extendable once) and the C1 visit visa do **not** permit work, including remote work, per 2026 enforcement statements [15][16].
- **E33G Remote Worker visa**: one year, for people employed by a foreign company, with proof of about USD 60,000 annual income; sources disagree on whether freelancers and business owners qualify and whether it renews [17][18].
- **C5A** content-creator visa: 60 days, extendable twice, needs an Indonesian sponsor [15].
- People talk about "visa runs", "my agent", KITAS, and the 183-day tax-residency line [16][19].

### Costs people quote
- Nomads.com (Nov 2026): about USD 3,442 a month for a nomad, USD 2,535 for a settled expat, USD 1,531 for a local standard; one-bed studio about USD 1,504 a month; coffee about USD 2 [4].
- A 2026 guide claims USD 1,500-2,500 a month is comfortable; reviewers argue rents rose sharply while build quality stayed poor [4].
- Scooter rental: maxi-scooter (NMAX/PCX) IDR 150-250k a day or 2.5-3.5m a month; smaller scooters less [20] **(single source)**.
- Warung meal IDR 20-35k in an older write-up; café bowls IDR 75-110k [21][22].
- Restaurants commonly add tax and service on top of menu prices [21].

## 2. Fitness culture

### The gym scene
- Canggu has an unusually dense gym market; one October 2025 guide lists 16, another (August 2026) lists 22 [7][23].
- **Body Factory Bali** (Jl. Nelayan 27): the social "see and be seen" gym popular with nomads; outdoor high-performance area, pool, dry sauna, jacuzzi, two ice baths, café; open about 6:00-22:00; day pass IDR 350-540k, monthly from IDR 2m; described by one travel guide as an official HYROX Training Club [7][23][24].
- **Nirvana Life** (Jl. Pantai Berawa 8): rice-field setting, 100+ classes, hammam, infrared sauna, two ice plunges at 4°C and 10°C; day pass about IDR 539-660k [7][23][25].
- **Obsidian** (Pererenan): billed as Indonesia's largest stand-alone gym; pool, onsen-style bath, cold plunge, outdoor HYROX zone; day pass IDR 440k [7][23].
- **Wanderlust Fitness Village** (Jl. Raya Padonan): CrossFit with daily WODs, HYROX conditioning, kickboxing, BJJ, 25 m pool [23][26].
- **S2S CrossFit** (Jl. Raya Semat, Tibubeneng): CrossFit and Olympic lifting in groups of up to 12, plus beach workouts [23].
- Budget and hardcore ends: **FLEX Gym** (day pass from IDR 100k), **Bull Gym** (no air-conditioning, powerlifters, "sweat, not selfie"), **Omni** (24-hour), **Fitness Plus** (IDR 500k a month) [7].
- Reviewers complain that gyms charge "American prices" [4].

### Run clubs
- **Rise & Run Bali** (Canggu and Uluwatu): Tuesday 6:15 am (4 km or 8 km loop), Thursday 6:15 am (speed session), Saturday 7:00 am; coffee after. Its founder has said the community grew to almost 100 expats a week [12][27].
- **Canggu Run Club**: international, easy routes, breakfast or coffee after; one schedule lists Tuesday and Thursday 5:00 pm from S2S CrossFit [27][28] **(times single source)**.
- **Flowerboy Run Club** (Seseh): Wednesday and Saturday at 6:00 am, 5 km and 10 km, free, about twenty regulars, starts and ends at the Flowerboy café [27][28].
- **Continuum Athletics** (Canggu): "Speed Tuesdays", "Threshold Thursdays", Sunday long run from Body Factory [12].
- **831 Coffee Club** (Kerobokan): about 150 people turned up to its first run [27].
- Routes people use: Seseh and Cemagi rice-field roads; Berawa-Pererenan-Echo Beach (8-10 km); Canggu-Seseh-Mengwi long run (12-15 km) [12][29].
- Heat rules: 26-32°C and 70-85% humidity; run at 6:00-7:00 am or 5:30-7:00 pm; newcomers need 7-10 days to acclimatise; rainy season (roughly November-March) pushes training indoors [29] **(single source)**.
- Target race: **Maybank Marathon**, Gianyar, 23 August 2026, 15th edition, 14,100 runners from 46 countries; marathon, half and 10K [30].

### HYROX
- Format: eight 1 km runs, each followed by one station, always in the same order: SkiErg (1,000 m), sled push (50 m), sled pull (50 m), burpee broad jumps (80 m), rowing (1,000 m), farmers carry (200 m), sandbag lunges (100 m), wall balls (100 reps). Men's Open loads include a 152 kg sled push, 2x24 kg farmers carry, 20 kg sandbag and 6 kg wall ball [31][32]. (Order and distances partly from general knowledge; the fixed order, SkiErg first and wall balls last were confirmed.)
- Divisions: individual Open and Pro, doubles (men's, women's, mixed) and relay [32].
- **No official HYROX race in Bali was found.** Indonesia's first was **HYROX Jakarta, 27-28 June 2026**, at NICE, PIK2: about 11,500 participants, called the largest debut in Asia Pacific; entries sold out; singles IDR 2.5m, doubles IDR 4.7m a pair [32][33].
- Other Asian races in the 2026 calendar: Bangkok (March), Singapore (April), Hong Kong and Incheon (May) [33]. Bangkok reported a record 20,000 participants in July 2026 coverage [34].
- In Bali the scene is training and simulations: the **Wanderlust HYROX Series 2025** at Wanderlust Fitness Village ran in May, August and October 2025 with pairs and individual formats; gyms run partner "sim" classes splitting reps across all 8 stations [26][35].
- One Indonesian guide claims Bali "usually hosts one race per season"; this contradicts everything else and should be ignored [29].
- Local media describe HYROX and marathon fever changing what Bali hotels offer guests [36].

### Recovery clubs
- Contrast therapy (sauna then ice bath) is standard at the big gyms, and recovery is sold as a social activity, not a private one [7][23].
- **Amo Spa**: steam, sauna, ice plunge pools, Epsom baths; described as having a social recovery scene [25].
- **RITE Bali** (Pererenan, opened January 2026): pool, cold plunge held at 7-9°C, sauna, jacuzzi, steam [37].
- **The Ark** (Pererenan): 3-hour thermal circuit; a review mentions a "Sauna Night" [37] **(single source)**.
- A private infrared sauna plus ice bath session elsewhere starts around IDR 450k [25].
- Evening DJ-and-sauna socials were not confirmed **(unverified)**.

### Padel
- Padel barely existed in Bali before COVID; one long-term blogger now calls it "religion" [8].
- **Bali Padel Academy** (north of Jl. Raya Canggu): six courts among rice fields, infinity pool, sauna, stadium court; about IDR 520k per court-hour (about 130k each split four ways) [8].
- **Jungle Padel** is the largest chain (Canggu shortcut area, Pererenan, Kerobokan, Kedungu, Seseh and more); **Island Padel** is in Canggu and Cemagi; **Bali Social Club** has seven courts [8][38].
- People book by app (Playtomic and club apps), leave a court "open" for strangers at a set rating, and play **Americano** tournaments: 16 players, rotating partners, about IDR 250k for two hours. Coaching costs IDR 800k-2m an hour [8].

### Muay Thai, yoga, surfing
- **Bali MMA** (Jl. Raya Padonan): a long-standing combat gym with striking, grappling, sparring and kids' BJJ. **SOMA Fight Club**: boxing, kickboxing, Muay Thai; day pass about IDR 300k. **Zealot Muay Thai**: single class about IDR 150k [23][39] **(prices may be dated)**.
- Yoga: **The Practice** (best known), **Samadi** (Ashtanga, also a Sunday market **(unverified)**), **Odyssey MVMT** [39].
- Surf as fitness: dawn sessions before work are the norm for surfers. Breaks: **Batu Bolong** and **Old Man's** are soft, longboard-friendly beginner waves and crowded in the morning; **Berawa** is the intermediate daily option; **Echo Beach** and **Pererenan** are faster and for advanced surfers [40].

### Food and supplements
- **Motion Cafe** is described as the area's main fitness café, known for protein-heavy meals; **Crate Cafe** bowls cost IDR 75-110k; **Nalu Bowls** smoothie bowls IDR 60-100k [22].
- Gym cafés sell protein shakes; Bull Gym has a protein bar [7].
- Macro-counted menus, meal-prep delivery, electrolyte sachets and creatine talk are common **(unverified; no page read confirmed specifics)**.
- One guide advises 500-600 ml of electrolyte drink per hour of hard training in the heat [29].

### A serious training day (composite from the above)
- 5:15 wake; 6:00-6:15 run club or solo intervals before the heat.
- 7:30 coffee and breakfast with the group.
- Midday or 4:00-5:00 pm second session: strength or a HYROX class (sleds, SkiErg, wall balls).
- 5:30-6:30 pm sauna and ice bath, often with the same people.
- One long run on Sunday; one sim or partner session a week; padel or surf as "active recovery".

## 3. Travellers and surfers

- **Backpackers**: cluster around Batu Bolong, which has the highest concentration of hostels. Day: surf lesson or beach in the morning, cheap brunch, sunset on the sand, then Old Man's or Sand Bar. One guide mentions a big beer-pong night at Old Man's every Wednesday [41] **(Wednesday detail single source)**.
- **Surf travellers**: check the tide, surf at dawn, eat, nap, surf again late afternoon. Beginners take 90-minute to 2-hour lessons at Batu Bolong for about USD 25-34 or IDR 425-500k, board, rash vest and zinc included; instructors move lessons between Batu Bolong, Echo Beach and Berawa by conditions [40][42].
- **Long-stay nomads**: villa or coliving, scooter, gym membership, a fixed café. Many reviewers say 1-3 months is the sweet spot and long-termers eventually leave [4].
- **Influencers and creators**: film in gyms, cafés and beach clubs. Since 2026, paid or bartered promotion on a tourist visa is treated as illegal work and immigration monitors social media, so creators are warier [15][16].
- **Families**: use FINNS Recreation Club (pools, sports, padel) and child-friendly gyms; Bali MMA runs kids' BJJ [23][38] **(family routines otherwise unverified)**.
- **Day-trippers**: FINNS Beach Club reportedly takes about 4,500 visitors a day [3].
- **Where groups overlap**: sunset at Batu Bolong and Echo Beach (hundreds of beanbags, coconuts and Bintang); cafés at brunch; run clubs; padel open matches; gym recovery areas; the weekly tech meetup [8][27][41].

## 4. Locals who work in this world

- **Pay context**: Badung regency (which includes Canggu) has Bali's highest minimum wage, IDR 3,791,002 a month for 2026, up 7.2%; the provincial floor is IDR 3,207,459. A café bowl at IDR 100k is roughly a quarter of a day's minimum wage [43].
- **Baristas and café staff**: mostly young Indonesians, many from other parts of Bali or from Java and other islands **(unverified)**; they learn regulars' names and orders.
- **Surf instructors**: local men who grew up reading these tides; they push beginners onto waves at Batu Bolong, carry the boards, and confirm lesson times the day before depending on the tide [40][42].
- **Scooter-rental owners**: small family businesses. A 23-year-old Canggu rental owner told AFP in 2024 that Canggu was getting busier and losing its tranquillity and greenery [44].
- **Warung owners**: family-run eateries serving nasi campur, nasi goreng and mie goreng; "bungkus" means takeaway [45].
- **Gojek and Grab drivers**: green jackets and helmets; cheaper than local taxis (reported as about half the price). Some banjar (village councils) and local taxi co-operatives bar app drivers from picking up in their area, so a driver may cancel or ask you to walk to the main road. Local drivers say app fares undercut them and that part of their income goes back to the community. A large protest took place in Denpasar in January 2025, followed by rules such as Bali-registered (DK) plates [46][47].
- **Villa staff**: housekeepers, pool men, gardeners and night security; many place daily offerings at the villa shrine **(unverified)**.
- **Gym coaches**: a mix of Indonesian trainers and foreign coaches; Bull Gym's trainers are described as powerlifting champions [7].
- **Masseuses and spa therapists**: Balinese massage at street spas; price not confirmed **(unverified, commonly IDR 100-200k an hour)**.
- **Beach vendors**: sell coconuts, corn, sarongs and bracelets, and rent beanbags and umbrellas at sunset [41].
- **Lifeguards (Balawista)**: Badung has 16 posts from Tuban to Cemagi, including Canggu's beaches. Shifts normally start at 7:00 am (moved to 6:00 am in busy periods). Red flag means no swimming, but flags are not always posted. West-facing beaches like Canggu have strong rips, worst in the wet season. Units have jet skis and rubber boats [48][49].
- **Parking attendants (tukang parkir)**: wave scooters in and out, hand over a small ticket; official motorbike fee in Badung rose to IDR 2,000, with 3,000-4,000 sometimes charged [50].
- **Pecalang**: traditional village security in black-and-white checked sarongs who direct traffic during ceremonies **(unverified in sources read; widely known)**.
- **Women making offerings**: canang sari (small palm-leaf trays of flowers, rice and incense) are placed each morning at doors, shrines, shop fronts and crossings; visitors are asked not to step on them [51].
- **Banjar**: the neighbourhood association that organises ceremonies and settles local matters; ceremonies can close a road with little notice [19][51].

### Phrases a visitor hears
Indonesian (used with everyone):
- *Selamat pagi* / *siang* / *sore* / *malam*: good morning / midday / afternoon / evening [45]
- *Terima kasih* / *makasih*: thank you / thanks [45]
- *Sama-sama*: you're welcome (standard Indonesian)
- *Permisi*: excuse me [45]
- *Hati-hati*: take care, be careful (said when you ride off; standard Indonesian)
- *Pelan-pelan*: slowly, take it easy [45]
- *Mau ke mana?*: where are you going? (a friendly greeting, not prying; standard Indonesian)
- *Bisa*: can, possible; heard constantly [45]
- *Bungkus*: wrap it, takeaway [45]
- *Sudah makan?*: have you eaten? (a common way of saying hello) **(unverified)**
- *Tidak apa-apa*: no problem **(unverified)**
- *Macet*: traffic jam **(unverified)**
- *Bule*: foreigner, usually Western; neutral to teasing [14]

Balinese (earns a smile):
- *Om Swastiastu*: formal Balinese Hindu greeting and blessing [45]
- *Suksma* / *matur suksma*: thank you / thank you very much; reply *mewali* [45]
- *Rahajeng semeng*: good morning [45]
- *Rahajeng rawuh*: welcome [45]
- *Bli* / *Mbok*: brother / sister, polite way to address a Balinese man / woman [45]
- *Mepamit*: I'm taking my leave [45]

English lines commonly heard from workers: "Transport, boss?", "Yes, massage?", "Where you from?" **(unverified)**.

## 5. Daily rhythm of the founder-athlete

Composite from run-club timetables, gym hours, time-zone logic and first-person accounts [2][12][13][27][29]. No single source gives this full day.

- **05:15** Wake before sunrise (sunrise is around 6:00-6:30 all year). Roosters, first scooters, incense from morning offerings.
- **05:45** Scooter through near-empty lanes. Electrolytes, no breakfast.
- **06:00-07:15** Run club or track-style intervals on rice-field roads; or dawn surf at Batu Bolong or Berawa.
- **07:30-08:30** Coffee and protein breakfast with the group. Most real networking happens here.
- **08:30-09:00** Home, shower, laundry dropped off, check overnight messages from US clients.
- **09:00-12:30** Deep work at a coworking space or café before clients wake up. Indian clients come online late morning.
- **12:30-13:30** Lunch: warung nasi campur or a protein bowl. Short nap or reading in the worst heat.
- **13:30-16:00** Meetings begin as Europe starts (3-4 pm). Team stand-ups, sales calls.
- **16:00-17:30** Second session: strength, HYROX class or padel.
- **17:30-18:30** Sauna and ice bath; or sunset at the beach (sunset about 6:00-6:30 pm).
- **19:00-20:30** Dinner; once or twice a week a founder dinner, meetup or mastermind.
- **21:00-23:00** US calls for those with American clients (9 am New York is 9-10 pm Bali).
- **22:30-23:00** Sleep. Serious trainers skip the bars; music from venues may still carry until about 1 am.
- **Weekly shape**: Sunday long run; one sim session; Saturday social run; one full rest day; a ceremony day or heavy rain can cancel plans without warning.

## 6. Honest tensions

- **Traffic**: narrow single-lane roads, no public transport, and ever more cars and scooters. Canggu and Tibubeneng are often at gridlock. A 4.7 km link road is planned for late 2026; a West Gatsu-Canggu road not before late 2027; a Denpasar-Canggu tunnel reported delayed to 2028. Scooter crashes are described as the leading cause of nomad injury and death [4][44][52].
- **Overdevelopment**: the environmental group Walhi says 3-6% of farmland in Badung, Gianyar and Tabanan converts to commercial use each year. One 2026 article cites about 5,838 hectares of rice field lost between 2018 and 2023 and a 60% fall in Canggu farmland **(single source)**. Hotel-association officials say there is already too much accommodation, causing price wars [19][53].
- **September 2025 floods**: Bali's worst in years killed at least 14-18 people. The environment minister blamed waste and land conversion; the governor disputed land conversion as the cause in Denpasar but announced a halt on new permits on productive farmland. Whether and how it is enforced was not confirmed [53][54].
- **Water and waste**: tourism is reported to use over 65% of Bali's fresh water **(single source)**; the island's main landfill was closed and temporarily reopened in 2025; beaches collect rubbish in the wet season [19][53].
- **Bali belly**: traveller's diarrhoea, mostly bacterial (commonly E. coli). Estimates range from about 25% to 50% of visitors. Ice and tap water are the usual suspects; most cases pass alone, bad ones get IV fluids at a clinic [55].
- **Visa crackdown**: from April 2026 an immigration patrol task force (Dharma Dewata) has targeted Canggu, Ubud, Seminyak, Kerobokan and Uluwatu. One report counts 62 foreigners detained in three weeks. Anything with "economic value", including unpaid promotion or teaching a class, counts as work on a tourist visa. Penalties: fines, detention, deportation, multi-year bans [15][16] **(mostly travel-news sources; no official statement read)**.
- **Tourist levy**: IDR 150,000 per foreign visitor since February 2024; the province says only about 34% of 7.0 million foreign visitors paid in 2025 [56].
- **Noise**: a 2022 petition against "extreme noise" in Canggu drew over 7,000 signatures. Authorities set a 70 dB limit and 1 am closing; later tests found 75-85 dB near venues [57].
- **Costs**: landlords in nomad areas reportedly raised rents over 15% in a year; rising minimum wages feed into café prices. Locals on about IDR 3.2-3.8m a month are priced out of the areas they work in and commute further [19][43].
- **Locals' views**: mixed, not hostile. Lonely Planet describes "cautious tolerance" toward nomads. Many jobs depend on visitors, and community managers and hotel officials defend the economy. Anger focuses on bad behaviour (reckless riding, disrespect at temples, working illegally and undercutting locals), on lost farmland, and on planning rules that are ignored. Social-media pages name and shame badly behaved "bule" [14][19][44][53].
- **Ride-hailing conflict**: app drivers against local taxi co-operatives, described above [46][47].
- **Fair summary for the game**: the same boom that built the gyms and cafés pays local wages and also took the rice fields, clogged the roads and raised rents. A truthful world shows both: offerings on the pavement beside a construction site, a friendly driver who cannot enter the next village, a rice farmer between two villas.

### Player activities
1. Join the 6:15 am run club: choose the 4 km or 8 km loop, keep pace in the heat, earn the post-run coffee conversation.
2. Dawn surf at the beginner break: read the flags, paddle out, time a wave before work.
